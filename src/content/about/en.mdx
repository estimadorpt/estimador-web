## Our mission

estimador.pt is a data analysis platform that publishes transparent, probabilistic forecasts about Portugal. We believe the public benefits from rigorous, unbiased analysis, not just punditry and speculation.

Our goal is to bring the standards of modern statistical modelling to the Portuguese context, combining Bayesian methods with real data to answer relevant questions.

## What we do

**Economy** — in preparation
A consolidated read of Portuguese economic indicators: activity, labour market, inflation, recession risk and public accounts. Once it is published, each indicator will state whether it is an official figure or an estimate from our model, the period it refers to and its publication date. Until then, the explanations of how to read the indicators are available. See the [economy methodology](/en/economia/metodologia/).

**Population** — version 1.0.1, released 5 October 2026 (replacing version 1.0.0 of the same day)
To the best of our knowledge, the first open-access synthetic population to cover every parish in Portugal, generated from the 2021 Census. Generated people and households, not real people, families or addresses. At [/populacao](/en/populacao/) you can look up your parish, play the daily Mystery parish and download the microdata. See the [population methodology](/en/populacao/metodologia/).

**Liga Portugal** — published on a continuing basis
A Bayesian model that simulates thousands of possible seasons to forecast standings, title and relegation probabilities, and the impact of each matchday on the league. See the [football methodology](/en/desporto/liga/metodologia/).

**Presidential elections 2026** — archive
The forecasts we published for the 2026 presidential election, preserved as they were made. They are not updated, nor rewritten with the outcome. They include that cycle's polling analysis: the estimated house effect of each polling firm, the consistent lean each one showed towards certain candidates.

**Parliamentary elections 2025** — archive
The forecast model for the 2025 parliamentary election, with district-level seat projections using the D'Hondt method and the pollster house effects estimated at the time.

## Our approach

We use Bayesian statistical methods, which quantify uncertainty explicitly. Our forecasts produce probability distributions that reflect what we actually know, and what we do not, about the outcomes.

**Principles:**

- **Transparency:** open methodology, with documentation of the models and their assumptions.
- **Independence:** no external funding and no editorial pressure.
- **Accuracy:** we say how each forecast is evaluated, and claim only what is published. The Liga Portugal model is checked against actual results and bookmakers' closing odds, over past seasons, in [Model vs market](/en/desporto/liga/modelo/). An evaluation of the election forecasts against the results is not published.
- **Accessibility:** probabilistic forecasts presented so that any reader can follow them.

## Limitations and caveats

Probabilistic forecasting is uncertain by nature, which is why we present probabilities, not certainties. Our models account for several sources of uncertainty, but there are always unpredictable factors.

A forecast of 70% also means a 30% chance of the opposite. Read our forecasts as informed probabilistic assessments, not facts.
