On 19 June 2017, Lisbon’s Câmara (the city council) launched the first tender of its affordable rent programme, the Programa Renda Acessível: 126 flats in 16 municipal buildings on Rua de São Lázaro, which a private partner would refurbish and let for 25 years ([ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2017/06/19/lancado-primeiro-concurso-para-programa-renda-acessivel-em-lisboa/)). In January 2019 the Court of Auditors (Tribunal de Contas) refused to approve the contract; it approved it in April 2020, after the Câmara appealed ([ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2020/04/07/mais-de-um-ano-depois-tribunal-de-contas-da-luz-verde-as-rendas-acessiveis-em-s-lazaro/)). In December 2022 the concession, by then 131 homes, was “still awaiting the handover of the works, since the case has been in court since 2020”, the Câmara said ([ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2022/12/14/lisboa-desiste-dos-concursos-para-renda-acessivel-em-benfica-paco-da-rainha-e-parque-das-nacoes/), our translation).

In those five and a half years the mayor lost his absolute majority, the rules for short lets changed at least three times and Euribor went from negative to positive. After that, licensing itself changed, twice. This piece puts those facts together, with their dates and sources.

## A game with invented sites and real laws

The facts come from the timeline we compiled for *Aguarda parecer* (Awaiting opinion), a game in which you run a small developer in Lisbon from January 2015 to the 2029 local elections. The sites, the characters and the news stories about your company are invented; the laws and the dates they changed, each list’s seats on the Câmara, INE’s prices up to 2025 and Euribor are real. The game’s rules are a simplification, and their assumptions favour building dense. The odds and intervals the game shows are the game’s own, not estimador.pt forecasts, and no number in this piece comes from playing it. ## Licensing changed in 2024 and again in 2026

The planning Simplex (Decree-Law 10/2024) was published on 8 January 2024 and has applied, for the most part, since 4 March that year. It brought tacit approval to licensing: if the council does not decide in time, the application counts as granted. It abolished the building licence document (the alvará), replaced by the receipt for the fees, and created new cases of prior notice (comunicação prévia), in which a developer can no longer choose a licence instead ([Diário da República](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/decreto-lei/10-2024-836222484); [Sérvulo](https://www.servulo.com/pt/investigacao-e-conhecimento/Simplex-urbanistico-arranca-em-2024/8651/)).

On 29 May 2026 a revision of the planning and building code (RJUE) was published, Decree-Law 108/2026. Under prior notice, works may start once the fees are paid and the start of works is notified, and the council checks only afterwards ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/prt/news-information/alteracoes-ao-regime-juridico-da-urbanizacao-e-edificacao)). The revision was due to take effect on 3 August; Decree-Law 155-B/2026 postponed it to 1 October 2026, to give councils time to adapt their procedures and IT platforms ([Ordem dos Engenheiros](https://www.ordemdosengenheiros.pt/regiao-sul/pt/noticias/entrada-em-vigor-da-revisao-do-rjue-adiada-para-1-de-outubro-de-2026/)).

In between, since July 2026, building and refurbishment contracts for homes sold for up to €660,982 or let for up to €2,300 a month pay 6% VAT, where the procedure began between 25 September 2025 and the end of 2029 (Decree-Law 97/2026; [Observador](https://observador.pt/2026/05/20/pacote-fiscal-da-habitacao-ja-publicado-em-diario-da-republica-iva-da-construcao-a-6-valido-para-obras-iniciadas-desde-setembro-de-2025/)). Which rules apply to an application already filed depends on each law’s transitional provisions, which this piece does not summarise.

## Euribor went from −0.50% to 4.16% in 22 months

The monthly average of 12-month Euribor was −0.50% in December 2021 and 4.16% in October 2023, the peak of that cycle ([ECB](https://data.ecb.europa.eu/data/datasets/FM/FM.M.U2.EUR.RT.MM.EURIBOR1YD_.HSTA)). The European Central Bank had held its deposit rate at −0.50% since September 2019; it raised it to 0% on 27 July 2022 and, in ten rises, to 4% on 20 September 2023 ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/key_ecb_interest_rates/html/index.en.html)). The cost of building new housing rose by 12.2% on average in 2022 ([INE](https://www.ine.pt/xurl/ind/0011751)).

Prices in Lisbon kept rising. The median price per square metre of homes sold went from €3,531 in 2021 to €4,875 in 2025, without falling in any year; the median rent per square metre on new leases, from €11.34 to €16.88 ([INE, sales](https://www.ine.pt/xurl/ind/0012255); [INE, rents](https://www.ine.pt/xurl/ind/0014698)).

## The 2021 tenders for 1,008 homes ended without a contract

In April 2021 the Câmara approved tenders to have private partners build 1,008 homes on municipal land, some of them at affordable rents: 688 in Benfica, 85 at Paço da Rainha, in Arroios, and 235 in Parque das Nações. The tenders ran until May 2022, with several postponements, and the juries found none of the four bids fit to be awarded. On 14 December 2022 the Câmara decided not to award them and said it was designing “a new model that could be attractive to private developers, given the global context of uncertainty in the construction sector”. The Rua Gomes Freire concession, 92 homes, had been under way since 12 October 2022 ([ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2022/12/14/lisboa-desiste-dos-concursos-para-renda-acessivel-em-benfica-paco-da-rainha-e-parque-das-nacoes/), our translation).

The source does not say it was interest rates: the Câmara spoke of uncertainty in the construction sector.

## No mayor of Lisbon has had a majority since 2017

Lisbon’s Câmara has 17 seats. A proposal passes with more votes for than against: abstentions do not count and a tie goes to the mayor’s casting vote (Law 75/2013, Annex I, article 54; [PGDL](https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=1990&tabela=leis)).

- **29 September 2013**: PS, 11 seats, an absolute majority ([ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2017/10/02/medina-perde-maioria-absoluta-em-lisboa/)).
- **1 October 2017**: PS 8, CDS-PP 4, PSD 2, CDU 2, BE 1 ([ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2017/10/02/medina-perde-maioria-absoluta-em-lisboa/)). In November, BE’s councillor took on portfolios in an agreement with the PS ([Renascença](https://rr.pt/noticia/politica/2017/11/02/acordo-politico-em-lisboa-bloco-ganha-pelouros-da-educacao-e-saude/97261/)).
- **26 September 2021**: Novos Tempos (PSD/CDS-PP/MPT/PPM/Aliança) 7, Mais Lisboa (PS/Livre) 7, CDU 2, BE 1 ([Notícias ao Minuto](https://www.noticiasaominuto.com/politica/1840277/social-democrata-carlos-moedas-conquistou-lisboa-ao-ps)).
- **12 October 2025**: Por ti, Lisboa (PSD/CDS-PP/IL) 8; PS 4, Chega 2, Livre 1, BE 1 and PCP 1 ([ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2025/12/17/camara-de-lisboa-aprova-orcamento-de-1-345-milhoes-para-2026-com-votos-do-chega/); [RTP](https://www.rtp.pt/noticias/politica/autarquicas-chega-vence-por-tres-votos-cdu-em-lisboa-e-confirma-dois-vereadores_n1692067)).

From 2021 to 2025 the mayor’s coalition held 7 seats and the opposition 10. In December 2021 the Câmara approved, on a PS and Livre proposal, a halt to new short-let registrations in 14 parishes; in the Municipal Assembly, PSD and CDS voted against ([DN](https://www.dn.pt/dinheiro/lisboa-alojamento-local-dispara-382-14777796.html)). In November 2023 it rejected, for the third time, the property-transfer tax (IMT) exemption for buyers aged up to 35 that the mayor, Carlos Moedas, proposed; PS, Cidadãos por Lisboa, PCP, Livre and BE voted against ([ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2023/11/29/lisboa-volta-a-chumbar-isencao-de-imt-para-jovens-ate-35-anos/)).

Since 2025 the mayor’s coalition has held 8 seats and needs other parties’ votes, or their abstentions. The 2026 budget passed with 10 votes for, the coalition’s 8 and Chega’s 2, and 7 against ([ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2025/12/17/camara-de-lisboa-aprova-orcamento-de-1-345-milhoes-para-2026-com-votos-do-chega/)). On 29 July 2026 the first subdivision of the Matinha detailed plan, in Marvila, with 771 homes, passed with PS, Livre and BE against and PCP and Chega abstaining ([Observador](https://observador.pt/2026/07/29/lisboa-aprova-loteamento-da-unidade-de-execucao-da-matinha-com-771-fogos/)). Counting seats, that was the coalition’s 8 against 6; had the 3 abstentions been votes against, it would have been 9 against 8.

## Lisbon’s short-let rules changed in 7 of the 9 years since 2018

- **9 November 2018**: new registrations halted in Bairro Alto, Madragoa, Castelo, Alfama and Mouraria ([Público](https://www.publico.pt/2018/11/09/local/noticia/alojamento-local-suspenso-bairro-alto-alfama-mouraria-castelo-madragoa-1850388)).
- **7 November 2019**: the municipal regulation published, with absolute containment zones (the Baixa and the Avenida da Liberdade, Avenida da República and Avenida Almirante Reis axes; Bairro Alto and Madragoa; Castelo, Alfama and Mouraria; Colina de Santana) and relative ones (Graça and Bairro das Colónias) ([ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2019/11/07/novas-zonas-de-contencao-do-alojamento-local-em-lisboa-ja-estao-em-vigor-veja-o-mapa/)).
- **15 April 2022**: new registrations halted in 14 of the 24 parishes, those where short lets reach 2.5% of dwellings ([Jornal de Negócios](https://www.jornaldenegocios.pt/empresas/detalhe/suspensao-de-novos-registos-de-alojamento-local-em-lisboa-entrou-hoje-em-vigor); [ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2022/03/22/lisboa-aprova-suspensao-de-novos-registos-de-alojamento-local-em-14-freguesias/)).
- **2 June 2023**: Beato passes 2.5% and the halt reaches 16 of the 24 parishes ([ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2023/06/02/freguesia-lisboeta-do-beato-ultrapassa-racio-de-alojamento-local-e-ficam-suspensos-novos-registos/)).
- **7 October 2023**: the Mais Habitação package (Law 56/2023) halts new registrations of flats nationwide, with exceptions for interior regions ([Diário da República](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/lei/56-2023-222477692)).
- **1 November 2024**: Decree-Law 76/2024 lifts that halt and hands regulation back to the municipalities ([Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/pt/portugal/imobiliario/art/alteracoes-ao-regime-do-alojamento-local-1)).
- **5 November 2024**: the Municipal Assembly halts new registrations across the whole city, where short lets stand at 7.2% ([ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2024/11/05/assembleia-municipal-de-lisboa-aprova-suspensao-imediata-de-novos-registos-de-alojamento-local/)).
- **6 December 2025**: the new regulation takes effect, with absolute containment where short lets reach 10% of permanent dwellings and relative containment between 5% and 10% ([Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/global/real-estate/art/amendment-lisbon-municipal-regulation-short-term-letting); [Observador](https://observador.pt/2025/12/02/assembleia-municipal-de-lisboa-aprova-novo-regulamento-do-alojamento-local/)).
- **6 April 2026**: 6,765 registrations cancelled, mostly for want of proof of insurance, leaving 11,774; Santa Maria Maior, Misericórdia and Santo António in absolute containment, São Vicente, Arroios and Estrela in relative containment ([ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2026/04/06/lisboa-ajusta-niveis-de-contencao-do-alojamento-local-apos-limpeza-de-registos/)).
