## How does the model work?

The model estimates an attacking and a defensive strength for each club. It fits them to two signals from every match: goals scored and conceded, modelled jointly (a bivariate Poisson, which lets the two teams' goals in the same match be related), and shots on target, which say more about who dominated a match than a 1-0 scoreline does.

The data is the last four seasons of the Primeira Liga and Liga 2, together, so promoted clubs arrive with a record. Each match counts for less the older it is: a match from about 23 months ago is worth half of one played today. Each club's starting point is its squad value (Transfermarkt), measured season by season, so old results are read against the squad the club had at the time.

The model is refitted from scratch after every matchday, with every match played so far.

## The simulations

With the strengths estimated, we simulate every remaining match <Sims /> times. Each simulation first draws plausible strengths for every club, within the model's uncertainty, and then the goals of each match, with home advantage. Each simulation produces a complete final table.

The probabilities we show come directly from these simulations. If a team finishes first in 51% of simulations, we say it has a 51% chance of winning the title: it is a count of how often each outcome happens. Final points come with a 90% range: the points total lands inside it in 90% of simulations.

## What counts as relegation?

On our pages, relegation means finishing 17th or 18th: going straight down to Liga 2. The 16th-placed club plays off against Liga 2's third and can go down too, but that place is not included in the relegation probability we show.

## Expected points (xPts)

Expected points come from each match's xG, from SofaScore. For every match, both teams' xG is turned, with a Poisson distribution, into win, draw and loss probabilities, and from there into expected points: three times the win probability plus the draw probability. Summed over the season, they give each team's xPts.

xPts do not use the forecasting model: they are an independent read. The gap between real points and xPts mixes finishing, goalkeeping and chance, so it is not a clean split between luck and skill.

## The simulator

The [simulator](/en/desporto/liga/simulador/) uses the same simulations. When you pick a result for one of next matchday's games, the probabilities become those of the simulations in which that result happened: a conditional probability, not a new run of the model. An unlikely result has fewer simulations behind it, so its probabilities are noisier. That is why the simulator works with one result at a time.

## Decisive matches

To measure the weight of each match, we fix the result (home win, draw or away win) and simulate the rest of the season. We compare the title probability, or the relegation probability, in each scenario. The matches where the gap between the best and worst case is largest are the most decisive.

## Key matches per team

For each team chasing the title or at risk of relegation, we compare two things for each match it has left: how often it wins that match across all simulations, and how often it wins it in the simulations where it reaches its goal. The bigger the gap, the more essential that win is.

## How do we measure players?

The [players page](/en/desporto/liga/jogadores/) publishes several separate metrics, one per dimension of the game, because a goalkeeper and a forward cannot be measured with the same number. Each comes from its own Bayesian model, fitted to individual Liga appearances <PlayerSeasons />, with a <PlayerMinMinutes />-minute minimum:

- **Finishing**: goals per 90 minutes above a replacement-level player (SAR, skill above replacement), adjusted for minutes, opponent and home advantage.
- **Attacking contribution**: the same calculation, with goals and assists together.
- **Contested possession**: the probability of winning aerial and ground duels, for defenders and midfielders.
- **Goalkeepers**: cross intervention and sweeping. Shot-stopping does not separate keepers over three seasons of data, so it has no list.

Each value comes with a 90% credible interval: the model puts a 90% probability on the true value lying inside it. With few minutes, the value is pulled toward replacement level and the interval widens. The page also shows each fit's technical checks (whether the algorithm converged); they sit in a separate box for anyone who wants them.

## What have we evaluated, and what not?

- **Evaluated**: the win, draw or loss forecast for each game of the next matchday, against the market's closing line, over past seasons. The result is on [model vs market](/en/desporto/liga/modelo/).
- **Evaluated**: the 90% ranges for final points (section above).
- **Partly checked**: the title probabilities of the team in front, which the model tends to underrate (section above).
- **Not evaluated**: the relegation and top-three probabilities, the decisive matches, the key matches per team and the simulator.

## What is left out?

The model gives each club one fixed strength for the rest of the season and does not use:

- January transfers and other mid-season squad changes;
- injuries, suspensions and starting line-ups;
- fixture congestion and motivation;
- managerial changes.

Forecasts are updated after every matchday. The files are on [open data](/en/desporto/liga/dados/).
