# Methodology & trust — Synthetic Population of Portugal

The *Synthetic Population of Portugal* is a statistical portrait of the Portuguese population, parish
by parish (freguesia) — **generated, not copied**. It is not the Census, it does not replace INE, and
it describes no real person. Its purpose is to answer cross-tabulations INE cannot release under
statistical confidentiality, while staying faithful to the official counts, and to serve as an open
data layer others can reuse.

The governing principle is the same as everywhere else on estimador.pt: **every number says what it is
and what it is not**. When a result is modest we say so; when it is a reference figure and not yet
national, it is labelled as such; where error exists, we publish it — parish by parish — rather than
hide it.

---

## What it is

It is, **as far as we know**, the first open-access synthetic population covering **every Portuguese
parish**, generated from the **2021 Census**. The phrase carries "as far as we know" on purpose: our
prior-art diligence supports the claim with the four qualifiers held together — *open access* + *every
parish* + *Portugal* + *2021 Census* — but it rests on public indexing, and internal or unindexed work
cannot be fully excluded. Drop those qualifiers and the phrase stops being defensible.

**These people are not real.** Every person and household was generated by a generative model — a
*variational autoencoder* — from the 2021 Census distributions. No record corresponds to a real
person, family, or address, and the dataset contains no *verbatim* Census respondents.

**How it is positioned.** Open synthetic populations are already a recognised public good: the United
Kingdom has the *SIPHER Synthetic Population* (MSOA level), the United States *RTI SynthPop™*
(block-group level), and in 2025 Ireland released its first open synthetic population (Electoral
Divisions). All are built with *reweighting / iterative proportional fitting / combinatorial
optimisation*. Portugal had no equivalent — only synthetic populations of the Lisbon area used as
internal inputs to transport simulations (not published as data and not at parish resolution). Our
contribution is twofold: **it is the first at parish level for the whole country, and it is generated
by a deep generative model**, not by reweighting. **It functions as a foundation layer for demographic
analysis** — a layer on which cross-tabs and models are built, not an official figure.

---

## Where the data comes from

Two sources, both from INE, playing different roles:

- **The 2021 Census margins** — the marginal distributions INE publishes at parish level (sex, age,
  education, labour status, marital status, and others). These are the **target**: it is to them that
  the model is calibrated, parish by parish, through **14 INE small-area constraint tables**.
- **The Public Use File (PUF / seed)** — the 2021 Census Public Use File, used **once** to pre-train
  the generative model. It is not redistributed in anything we publish (see *Licence*).

The path, in one sentence: **a VAE is pre-trained once on the national microdata → it is fine-tuned per
parish, fitting the latent space to the 14 INE margins (the decoder stays frozen) → autoregressive
decode → P1 selection + G1 guided resampling → age coherence → evaluation** against the INE margins.
The decoder is trained once and frozen; what changes from parish to parish is only the latent
fine-tune.

---

## How it is generated — generated, not copied

This is the claim that carries the whole product, and it is measurable. The classic alternative —
*Simulated Annealing / Combinatorial Optimization* (SA/CO) — is a **donor replay**: it picks and
recombines real Census records, and so **99.07% of its people are exact copies** of a real respondent.
It scores about 0.01 better on SRMSE precisely **because it copies**.

Our engine is generative. On the 13 core attributes, only **~14% of households** and **~32% of people**
match a seed record in full; **about 86% of households match no seed household**. We pay ~0.01 of SRMSE
for not copying — and it is a price we choose to pay, because it is what makes the claim "these are not
real people" true.

**Post-decode operations.** Not everything is read straight off the model; there are downstream
operations, and we distinguish them by tier, each with a different theoretical standing. They are not
all "repair":

| Tier | What it does | Standing | Fraction touched |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — In-model constraints** | Impossible combinations made impossible at the source (*logit* masking) | Correct by construction | not post-decode |
| **1 — Derivation** | Composition counts and reference-person attributes recomputed from the people themselves | Standard ML practice (single source of truth, not "repair") | deterministic |
| **2 — Principled resampling** | P1 (oversample + greedy pick) and G1 (guided resampling) — the model proposes, the parish margins select (*sampling-importance-resampling*) | Sound Monte Carlo; every shipped household was model-proposed | G1 resamples ~11% of non-reference people |
| **3 — Off-manifold editing** | Age-coherence swaps — outputs the model never proposed, created toward empirical targets | The weakest tier; it is scaffolding, not architecture | small; logged per parish (`n_swaps`), with an off switch and a target trending to 0 |

Tiers 0–2 are **architecture**, not apology. Tier 3 is the only one that leaves the model's support: it
always carries a root-cause item in the backlog, a shrink metric (`n_swaps` per parish, which should
trend to zero across versions), and an off switch so evaluation can be run with and without it. The
novelty rate above is measured **after all of these operations** — it is what ships, not the raw
decoder output.

## What it validates against

We measure fit with the **SRMSE** (standardised root-mean-square error) of each synthetic table against
the corresponding INE margin, parish by parish. The numbers below are **reference figures — 50 parishes
of the Aveiro district**, the frozen configuration. **They are not yet the national result**; the
national evaluation replaces them when complete (which is why the scorecard is a *feed* with scope and
status, and swaps in on its own).

| Metric | Value (reference) | Scope | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median error per person (SRMSE) | **0.089** (own-50) / **0.092** (common-48) | 50 parishes (Aveiro) | ≤ 0.10 |
| Marital status | **0.062** | 50 parishes | ≈ SA/CO (0.063) |
| Child deficit (under 15) | **−1.7%** | Aveiro | ≥ −10% |
| Structural violations | **0** | 50 parishes | 0 |

This was the first VAE configuration to clear the 0.10 threshold on the per-person median.
**Generalization was tested out of sample** on a set of 50 stratified parishes (including islands and
the Flores group), with acceptance bands **pre-registered by parish size**. Every size stratum passes
its band; the smallest parishes (fewer than 500 residents) pay a tail penalty — and are **suppressed**
in any case (see below).

**The island child deficit — found, diagnosed, and fixed.** In an earlier version, the parishes of the
Azores and Flores **under-generated children (−13%)**, and this was a **real blocker** to island
publication. We diagnosed the cause — it was mainly the **mid-to-large parishes with a high INE child
target**, not the tiny ones — and fixed it with a household-composition-aware decoder (`v7_d2fix_cs`)
and a dual-ascent child constraint. On the **relaunch battery** (the same 50 out-of-sample parishes),
the deficit moved to **Azores −7.6% / Flores −8.7%**, both **inside the −10% gate**; every stratum sits
in band, with **16/16 coverage and 0 structural violations**. The result: the earlier conditional hold
("hold the islands") was **upgraded to an unconditional GO, islands included**. These are the
relaunch-battery figures (50 out-of-sample parishes); the national run (R=3) is now underway and its numbers
replace these reference figures when complete.

## Privacy

The novelty rate proves **"not copied"**. It does not prove **"not re-identifiable"** — a record can be
novel (no exact twin in the seed) yet sit so close to one real respondent that it discloses something
about them. Re-identification is a matter of **distance and inference**, not of exact copying. So we run
the three empirical tests the statistical-disclosure-control literature uses. The measurements below are
from **one district (50 Aveiro parishes)** and **must be re-run on the national evaluation** before the
claim holds for the national product.

A structural point frames everything: the 13 attributes are coarse categoricals, and the real
population is enormously redundant on them — **74.6% of real respondents share their entire 13-attribute
record with at least one other real respondent**. The right question is never "is a synthetic record
close to a real one?" (they all are), but "**is it *closer* to the seed than the seed is to itself?**".

- **Distance to closest record (DCR).** A synthetic person is, at the median, **2** attributes from the
  nearest real record; a real respondent is **0** from another real respondent. That is, synthetic
  records are **farther** from real data than real records are from each other — no memorization signal.
- **Membership inference.** Deciding whether a specific person was in the training data is no easier from
  the synthetic population than from an independent *real* sample: the extra signal attributable to the
  synthesis is **≈ 0** (households +0.001; persons −0.059).
- **Attribute disclosure.** Knowing someone's parish, sex, age and marital status and reading income off
  the synthetic data scores **0.812** — statistically identical to an oracle trained on real data
  (**0.813**). The gain over the public baseline is **population structure**, not an individual secret;
  had the model memorized rare individuals, it would beat the real oracle, and it does not.

Against the three tests of **WP29 Opinion 05/2014** (*singling out*, *linkability*, *inference*) the
measured risk is **low** on all three, and every released record is fully generated — there is no data
subject behind a synthetic row, which frames the output as **anonymous information** under Recital 26 of
the GDPR. This is a **defensible technical position, not a legal determination**. As a concrete
safeguard, **parishes with fewer than 500 residents are suppressed** from the microdata (they remain
only in the quality file, with the municipality to aggregate to), and we do not publish unvalidated
attributes (occupation, commuting).

## Limits and scope

- **Small-parish tail.** The smallest parishes are the hardest for any method and the ones we suppress.
  In mid-quality parishes, read cross-tabs at the municipality level.
- **A single vintage.** Everything rests on the **2021 Census**. There is no time series; it is a
  snapshot of one moment.
- **Auditable, not fully reproducible.** The synthesis and evaluation code is open and the artifacts are
  timestamped, but the **seed is not redistributable** — an outsider cannot fetch it to reproduce the
  run from scratch. That is why we say *auditable*, not *fully reproducible*.
- **Model estimates are estimates.** The population **carries synthesis error**, which we publish parish
  by parish in the quality file. Where a number is the model's, it is labelled as the model's; where it
  is INE's, it is labelled as official.
